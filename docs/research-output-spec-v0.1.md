# Research Output Specification v0.1

A completed run should produce a reproducible research dossier.

## Required sections

1. **Executive answer** — concise answer to the original request.
2. **Question interpretation** — normalized question, ambiguities and constraints.
3. **Scope** — included/excluded domain, population/context/time limits when relevant.
4. **Methodology** — research design selected, why it fits, and quality controls.
5. **Research map** — subquestions and their completion state.
6. **Evidence review** — important sources and evidence, classified by source type.
7. **Claim matrix** — claim, supporting evidence, contradicting evidence, confidence and provenance.
8. **Synthesis** — integrated explanation; distinguish observation from inference.
9. **Contradictions/debate** — unresolved or competing findings.
10. **Uncertainty** — what is known, probable, unknown and sensitive to assumptions.
11. **Limitations** — limitations of evidence, retrieval, methodology and the engine.
12. **Conclusion** — direct answer to the initial question.
13. **Recommendations/perspectives** — only when warranted by the research.
14. **References** — complete traceable references.
15. **Research log** — iterations, searches/actions and stopping rationale.

## Evidence language

Use calibrated language. Prefer:
- "the evidence supports..." when evidence converges;
- "evidence is mixed..." when credible sources disagree;
- "there is insufficient evidence..." when a conclusion cannot be supported;
- "this is an inference..." when the statement is derived rather than directly observed.

Never convert absence of evidence into evidence of absence without justification.

## Auditability
Every important statement in the final synthesis should be traceable to claim IDs and ultimately to source/evidence IDs whenever possible.
